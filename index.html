Some cafes are built to sell coffee. Prithvi Cafe was built around an idea: that people who make art, and people who come to watch it, should have somewhere to sit together afterwards. Tucked beside Prithvi Theatre in Juhu, it has been that place for decades, and it still feels more like a green room than a restaurant.

Its own tagline says it plainly: where art meets food. This post walks through what that means in practice, from the story that started it to the dishes on the table and the evenings on the calendar.

An artistic legacy

The cafe grows out of the vision of Prithviraj Kapoor, the pioneer of Indian theatre whose name the whole complex carries. Prithvi Theatre opened in Juhu in 1978, founded by his son Shashi Kapoor and Jennifer Kendal Kapoor, and the cafe has been part of its story ever since. It describes itself as the living heartbeat of Mumbai's theatrical soul, and the three chapters below show how it got there.

The Signature Edit: what to eat

The menu is short on gimmicks and long on ideas. Three dishes are the ones to start with. Each takes something familiar and gives it a small stage of its own.

  • Pav Bhaji Fondue

    ₹450
    Iconic

    A contemporary reimagination of heritage street flavour. If you grew up on Mumbai's pav bhaji, this is the same comfort served as a shared, molten centrepiece.

  • Chicken Kheema Pav

    ₹525
    Chef's edit

    Slow-cooked minced chicken infused with secret theatre spices, served with pav. The kind of dish that rewards a slow second helping.

  • Alfredo Pasta

    ₹480
    Popular

    Velvety white-sauce pasta with a hint of roasted garlic. The easy order for anyone at the table who wants something familiar.

Ordering tipShare the fondue as an opener, then split one kheema pav and one pasta. That covers street food, spice and comfort for roughly ₹1,455 before extras.

Atmosphere of wonder

The food is only half the pitch. The setting does the rest, and it changes noticeably between afternoon and night.

Fairy-lit evenings

The outdoor area glows with lights and lanterns after dark, with rustic wooden structures around you.

Theatrical drinks

Cocktails are prepared under dramatic lighting, so even the bar feels staged.

Gold-leaf desserts

Finish with a decadent dessert, some finished with gold leaf.

Inside, the theatre's side of the story shows up in vintage posters and plush seating. It reads as lived-in rather than designed, which is the point.

The verdict

Prithvi Cafe works because it never tries to separate the meal from the memory. You come for a fondue or a plate of kheema pav, and you leave having sat inside a piece of Mumbai's cultural history. Book a table, check the programme, and let the evening run long.
